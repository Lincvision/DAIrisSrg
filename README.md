Existing CNN-based iris segmentation methods commonly assume that training and application stages share the same data distribution and modality setting, thus their performance may decline substantially on open-domain iris datasets unseen before. Furthermore, annotating pixel-wise labels is labor-intensive and time-consuming, resulting in the limited applicability of these methods in realistic scenarios. To this end, we propose a generic domain adaptation iris segmentation framework including a domain-sensitive feature whitening strategy and a pseudo-label refinement strategy, which utilize domain-invariant representation for better generalization. Our method does not require a specific network structure and can be flexibly incorporated into existing methods, proving its universality. Meanwhile, our method fine-tunes the baseline model during the target-training process without accessing the source data and target annotations, endowing its security. Thus, it is suitable for deploying in a real-world scenario. The paper should be of interest to readers in the areas of iris segmentation and iris recognition. We hope that the proposed model will foster further research in iris segmentation.
